\section{Q1: Integral-Slot Winding Design}

For this question, two winding designs are done namely full-pitched and 11/12 short-pitched windings. The given machine parameters for these windings are:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Number of poles: $p = 6$ 
    \item Number of stator slots: $Q = 72$
    \item Number of phases: $m = 3$
    \item Winding configuration: Double-layer
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Derived Parameters:}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Slots per pole per phase ($q$):}
    \[ q = \frac{Q}{p \cdot m} = \frac{72}{6 \times 3} = 4\]

    \item \textbf{Electrical slot angle ($\alpha_e$):}
    \[\alpha_e = \frac{p \times 180^\circ}{Q} = \frac{6 \times 180^\circ}{72} = 15^\circ/\text{slot}\]

    \item \textbf{Mechanical slot angle ($\alpha_m$):}
    \[\alpha_m = \frac{360^\circ}{Q} = \frac{360^\circ}{72} = 5^\circ/\text{slot}\]

    \item \textbf{Pole pitch ($\tau_p$):}
    \[\tau_p = \frac{Q}{p} = \frac{72}{6} = 12 \text{ slots}\]
\end{enumerate}


\subsection{Configuration A: Full-Pitched Winding}

\subsubsection{Winding Diagram}

For a full-pitched winding, the coil span equals the pole pitch: $y = \tau_p = 12$ slots, corresponding to $180^\circ$ electrical. In a double-layer winding, the top layer conductor in slot $i$ is connected to the bottom layer conductor in slot $i + y$. This means the bottom layer of slot $i$ serves as the return side of the coil whose top side resides in slot $i - y$. Since the phase sequence repeats every 12 slots with alternating polarity, the bottom layer ends up identical to the top layer at each slot position.

The phase sequence follows the order $A,\; {-C},\; B,\; {-A},\; C,\; {-B}$, giving a balanced 3-phase layout across the 24-slot pole-pair:

\subsubsection{Winding Factor Calculations}

The general formulas for the $n$-th harmonic are:
\[
    k_{d,n} = \frac{\sin\!\left(\dfrac{n \cdot q \cdot \alpha_e}{2}\right)}{q \cdot \sin\!\left(\dfrac{n \cdot \alpha_e}{2}\right)}
    \qquad
    k_{p,n} = \sin\!\left(\frac{n \cdot y_e}{2}\right)
    \qquad
    k_{w,n} = k_{d,n} \cdot k_{p,n}
\]

where $y_e = 180^\circ$ electrical for full pitch.

\paragraph{Fundamental ($n = 1$):}
\[
    k_{d,1} = \frac{\sin(4 \times 15^\circ / 2 \times 1)}{4\,\sin(15^\circ/2 \times 1)}
            = \frac{\sin(30^\circ)}{4\,\sin(7.5^\circ)}
            = \frac{0.500}{4 \times 0.131} = \frac{0.500}{0.522} = 0.958
\]
\[
    k_{p,1} = \sin(90^\circ) = 1.000 \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad k_{w,1} = 0.958
\]

\paragraph{3rd Harmonic ($n = 3$):}
\[
    k_{d,3} = \frac{\sin(3 \times 30^\circ)}{4\,\sin(3 \times 7.5^\circ)}
            = \frac{\sin(90^\circ)}{4\,\sin(22.5^\circ)}
            = \frac{1.000}{4 \times 0.383} = \frac{1.000}{1.531} = 0.653
\]
\[
    k_{p,3} = \sin(3 \times 90^\circ) = \sin(270^\circ) = -1.000 \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad k_{w,3} = -0.653
\]

\paragraph{5th Harmonic ($n = 5$):}
\[
    k_{d,5} = \frac{\sin(5 \times 30^\circ)}{4\,\sin(5 \times 7.5^\circ)}
            = \frac{\sin(150^\circ)}{4\,\sin(37.5^\circ)}
            = \frac{0.500}{4 \times 0.609} = \frac{0.500}{2.435} = 0.205
\]
\[
    k_{p,5} = \sin(5 \times 90^\circ) = \sin(450^\circ) = 1.000 \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad k_{w,5} = 0.205
\]

\subsection{Configuration B: 11/12 Short-Pitched Winding}

\subsubsection{ Winding Diagram}

For the $11/12$ short-pitched design, the coil span is reduced by one slot: $y = 11$ slots. In electrical degrees:
\[
    y_e = 11 \times 15^\circ = 165^\circ \text{ electrical}
\]
The top layer is identical to the full-pitch case. However, the bottom layer of slot $i$ now carries the return conductor of the coil whose top side is at slot $i - 11$. This shifts the bottom layer by one slot relative to the full-pitch arrangement:

\subsubsection{Winding Factor Calculations}

The distribution factor does not depend on the pitch which makes the distribution factor identical for both configurations. Only the pitch factor changes:

\[k_{p,n} = \sin\!\left(\frac{n \times 165^\circ}{2}\right) = \sin(n \times 82.5^\circ)\]

\paragraph{Fundamental ($n = 1$):}
\[k_{p,1} = \sin(82.5^\circ) = 0.991 \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad k_{w,1} = 0.958 \times 0.991 = 0.949\]

\paragraph{3rd Harmonic ($n = 3$):}
\[k_{p,3} = \sin(247.5^\circ) = -0.924 \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad k_{w,3} = 0.653 \times (-0.924) = -0.604\]

\paragraph{5th Harmonic ($n = 5$):}
\[k_{p,5} = \sin(412.5^\circ) = \sin(52.5^\circ) = 0.793 \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad k_{w,5} = 0.205 \times 0.793 = 0.163\]

\subsection{Discussion}

The following conclusions can be drawn:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Moving from full-pitch to 11/12 short-pitch reduces the fundamental winding factor minimally, from $k_{w,1} = 0.958$ to $0.949$ (a decrease of 0.9\%). The machine sustains nearly the same torque and back-EMF capability.

    \item Third harmonic slightly decreased (7.5\%) for short-pitch configuration. However, in a balanced 3-phase machine the triplen harmonics ($n = 3, 9, 15, \ldots$) cancel in the \emph{line-to-line} voltage and do not appear in the output regardless of their winding factor. Yet, they may still contribute to stator core losses and noise.

    \item Short-pitching reduces $|k_{w,5}|$ from $0.205$ to $0.163$ ( approximately 20\% reduction). The pitch factor $k_{p,5}$ drops from $1.000$ to $0.793$. This is the primary benefit of the 11/12 pitch: it meaningfully attenuates the 5th space harmonic, which is responsible for additional losses in the machine.

    \item Short-pitching always involves a trade-off between harmonic suppression and fundamental EMF and torque. In this case the 11/12 pitch is a very efficient compromise, it achieves noticeable 5th harmonic reduction at the cost of less than 1\% in fundamental performance.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Q2: Fractional-Slot Winding Design}

The assigned design has the following parameters:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Number of stator slots: $N_s = 15$
    \item Number of rotor poles: $N_m = 14$
    \item Number of phases: $m = 3$
    \item Slots per pole per phase: $N_{spp} \approx 0.357$
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Phase Angles of Induced Voltage per Slot}

Using the star-of-slots method, the 360° electrical circle is divided into $2m = 6$ zones of $60°$ each. Each slot phasor is assigned to the phase zone it falls into; if the phasor falls in a negative zone, the coil side is wound in the reverse direction:

\begin{table}[h!]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{ccc}
\toprule
\textbf{Zone} & \textbf{Range} & \textbf{Assignment} \\
\midrule
A$+$ & $[330°,\; 30°)$ & Phase A, positive \\
C$-$ & $[30°,\;  90°)$ & Phase C, negative \\
B$+$ & $[90°,\; 150°)$ & Phase B, positive \\
A$-$ & $[150°,\;210°)$ & Phase A, negative \\
C$+$ & $[210°,\;270°)$ & Phase C, positive \\
B$-$ & $[270°,\;330°)$ & Phase B, negative \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Star-of-slots zone assignment ($60°$ zones).}
\end{table}

Applying the zone assignment to all 15 slots gives exactly 5 slots per phase, confirming a balanced winding:

The resulting slot groupings are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Phase A:} Slots 1$(+)$, 2$(-)$, 3$(+)$, 14$(+)$, 15$(-)$
    \item \textbf{Phase B:} Slots 4$(+)$, 5$(-)$, 6$(+)$, 7$(-)$, 8$(+)$
    \item \textbf{Phase C:} Slots 9$(+)$, 10$(-)$, 11$(+)$, 12$(-)$, 13$(+)$
\end{itemize}


\subsection{Phasor Diagram and Winding Factor}

The winding factor for the $n$-th harmonic is computed as the ratio of the vector sum magnitude to the arithmetic sum magnitude of the slot phasors for one phase:
\[
    k_{w,n} = \frac{\left|\displaystyle\sum_{\text{Phase}} \vec{e}_{k,n}\right|}{N_s / m}
\]
where each phasor $\vec{e}_{k,n}$ has unit magnitude and angle $n \cdot \varphi_k$ (negated for slots wound in the $-$ direction).

\paragraph{Fundamental ($n=1$):}

The Phase A phasors and their effective angles are:

\begin{table}[h!]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{cccc}
\toprule
\textbf{Slot} & \textbf{Sign} & \textbf{Raw angle $\varphi_k$} & \textbf{Effective phasor angle} \\
\midrule
 1 & $+$ & $  0°$ & $  0°$ \\
14 & $+$ & $ 24°$ & $ 24°$ \\
15 & $-$ & $192°$ & $ 12°$ \quad ($192° - 180°$) \\
 2 & $-$ & $168°$ & $348°$ \quad ($168° - 180° + 360°$) \\
 3 & $+$ & $336°$ & $336°$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Phase A phasors for $n=1$. The five phasors cluster tightly around $0°$, yielding a high winding factor.}
\end{table}

\[
    k_{w,1} = \frac{|\,\vec{e}_1 + \vec{e}_{14} + \vec{e}_{15} + \vec{e}_2 + \vec{e}_3\,|}{5}
            = \frac{4.783}{5} = \mathbf{0.957}
\]

\paragraph{3rd Harmonic ($n=3$):}

Each phasor angle is multiplied by 3. The Phase A phasors become $0°,\;72°,\;36°,\;324°,\;288°$:
\[
    k_{w,3} = \frac{3.236}{5} = \mathbf{0.647}
\]

\paragraph{5th Harmonic ($n=5$):}

Each phasor angle is multiplied by 5. The Phase A phasors become $0°,\;120°,\;60°,\;300°,\;240°$:
\[
    k_{w,5} = \frac{1.000}{5} = \mathbf{0.200}
\]

\paragraph{Balance verification:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Each phase occupies exactly 5 slots $= N_s/m = 15/3$. \checkmark
    \item The phase resultants at $n=1$ are at $0°$, $120°$, $-120°$ respectively, forming a balanced three-phase set. \checkmark
    \item The winding factor is identical for all three phases by symmetry. \checkmark
\end{itemize}


\subsection{Discussion}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{High fundamental winding factor:} $k_{w,1} = 0.957$ is comparable to well-optimised distributed windings. This is a key advantage of the 15-slot/14-pole combination and results from the tight angular clustering of the five Phase A phasors (all within $\pm 24°$ of the resultant direction).

    \item \textbf{3rd harmonic --- zero-sequence:} For $n=3$, all three phase resultants are co-phasal (pointing in the same direction). This makes the 3rd harmonic a zero-sequence component: it does not produce a rotating MMF wave and is cancelled in line-to-line voltage and in $\Delta$-connected windings. However, it may still drive circulating currents and iron losses in the stator yoke.

    \item \textbf{5th harmonic --- naturally suppressed:} $k_{w,5} = 0.200$ is small relative to $k_{w,1} = 0.957$ (ratio $\approx 0.21$). This is considerably lower than for a typical full-pitch distributed winding, confirming that FSCW inherently attenuates higher-order spatial harmonics.

    \item \textbf{No sub-harmonics:} Since $\gcd(N_s,\,N_m) = \gcd(15,\,14) = 1$, there are no sub-harmonic MMF components (i.e.\ no harmonics of order lower than $p=7$). This eliminates large parasitic radial forces and unbalanced magnetic pull, which is a major advantage of this slot/pole combination.
\end{enumerate}
