\section{Q1: Integral-Slot Winding Design}

For this question, two winding designs are done namely full-pitched and 11/12 short-pitched windings. The given machine parameters for these windings are:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Number of poles: $p = 6$ 
    \item Number of stator slots: $Q = 72$
    \item Number of phases: $m = 3$
    \item Winding configuration: Double-layer
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Derived Parameters:}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Slots per pole per phase ($q$):}
    \[ q = \frac{Q}{p \cdot m} = \frac{72}{6 \times 3} = 4\]

    \item \textbf{Electrical slot angle ($\alpha_e$):}
    \[\alpha_e = \frac{p \times 180^\circ}{Q} = \frac{6 \times 180^\circ}{72} = 15^\circ/\text{slot}\]

    \item \textbf{Mechanical slot angle ($\alpha_m$):}
    \[\alpha_m = \frac{360^\circ}{Q} = \frac{360^\circ}{72} = 5^\circ/\text{slot}\]

    \item \textbf{Pole pitch ($\tau_p$):}
    \[\tau_p = \frac{Q}{p} = \frac{72}{6} = 12 \text{ slots}\]
\end{enumerate}


\subsection{Configuration A: Full-Pitched Winding}

\subsubsection{Winding Diagram}

For a full-pitched winding, the coil span equals the pole pitch: $y = \tau_p = 12$ slots, corresponding to $180^\circ$ electrical. In a double-layer winding, the top layer conductor in slot $i$ is connected to the bottom layer conductor in slot $i + y$. This means the bottom layer of slot $i$ serves as the return side of the coil whose top side resides in slot $i - y$. Since the phase sequence repeats every 12 slots with alternating polarity, the bottom layer ends up identical to the top layer at each slot position.

The phase sequence follows the order $A,\; {-C},\; B,\; {-A},\; C,\; {-B}$, giving a balanced 3-phase layout across the 24-slot pole-pair:

\subsubsection{Winding Factor Calculations}

The general formulas for the $n$-th harmonic are:
\[
    k_{d,n} = \frac{\sin\!\left(\dfrac{n \cdot q \cdot \alpha_e}{2}\right)}{q \cdot \sin\!\left(\dfrac{n \cdot \alpha_e}{2}\right)}
    \qquad
    k_{p,n} = \sin\!\left(\frac{n \cdot y_e}{2}\right)
    \qquad
    k_{w,n} = k_{d,n} \cdot k_{p,n}
\]

where $y_e = 180^\circ$ electrical for full pitch.

\paragraph{Fundamental ($n = 1$):}
\[
    k_{d,1} = \frac{\sin(4 \times 15^\circ / 2 \times 1)}{4\,\sin(15^\circ/2 \times 1)}
            = \frac{\sin(30^\circ)}{4\,\sin(7.5^\circ)}
            = \frac{0.500}{4 \times 0.131} = \frac{0.500}{0.522} = 0.958
\]
\[
    k_{p,1} = \sin(90^\circ) = 1.000 \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad k_{w,1} = 0.958
\]

\paragraph{3rd Harmonic ($n = 3$):}
\[
    k_{d,3} = \frac{\sin(3 \times 30^\circ)}{4\,\sin(3 \times 7.5^\circ)}
            = \frac{\sin(90^\circ)}{4\,\sin(22.5^\circ)}
            = \frac{1.000}{4 \times 0.383} = \frac{1.000}{1.531} = 0.653
\]
\[
    k_{p,3} = \sin(3 \times 90^\circ) = \sin(270^\circ) = -1.000 \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad k_{w,3} = -0.653
\]

\paragraph{5th Harmonic ($n = 5$):}
\[
    k_{d,5} = \frac{\sin(5 \times 30^\circ)}{4\,\sin(5 \times 7.5^\circ)}
            = \frac{\sin(150^\circ)}{4\,\sin(37.5^\circ)}
            = \frac{0.500}{4 \times 0.609} = \frac{0.500}{2.435} = 0.205
\]
\[
    k_{p,5} = \sin(5 \times 90^\circ) = \sin(450^\circ) = 1.000 \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad k_{w,5} = 0.205
\]

\subsubsection{Winding Factor Calculations}
The distribution factor does not depend on the pitch which makes the distribution factor identical for both configurations. Only the pitch factor changes:

\[k_{p,n} = \sin\!\left(\frac{n \times 165^\circ}{2}\right) = \sin(n \times 82.5^\circ)\]

\paragraph{Fundamental ($n = 1$):}
\[k_{p,1} = \sin(82.5^\circ) = 0.991 \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad k_{w,1} = 0.958 \times 0.991 = 0.949\]

\paragraph{3rd Harmonic ($n = 3$):}
\[k_{p,3} = \sin(247.5^\circ) = -0.924 \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad k_{w,3} = 0.653 \times (-0.924) = -0.604\]

\paragraph{5th Harmonic ($n = 5$):}
\[k_{p,5} = \sin(412.5^\circ) = \sin(52.5^\circ) = 0.793 \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad k_{w,5} = 0.205 \times 0.793 = 0.163\]

\subsection{Discussion}
The following conclusions can be drawn:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Moving from full-pitch to 11/12 short-pitch reduces the fundamental winding factor minimally, from $k_{w,1} = 0.958$ to $0.949$ (a decrease of 0.9\%). The machine sustains nearly the same torque and back-EMF capability.

    \item Third harmonic slightly decreased (7.5\%) for short-pitch configuration. However, in a balanced 3-phase machine the triplen harmonics ($n = 3, 9, 15, \ldots$) cancel in the \emph{line-to-line} voltage and do not appear in the output regardless of their winding factor. Yet, they may still contribute to stator core losses and noise.

    \item Short-pitching reduces $|k_{w,5}|$ from $0.205$ to $0.163$ ( approximately 20\% reduction). The pitch factor $k_{p,5}$ drops from $1.000$ to $0.793$. This is the primary benefit of the 11/12 pitch: it meaningfully attenuates the 5th space harmonic, which is responsible for additional losses in the machine.

    \item Short-pitching always involves a trade-off between harmonic suppression and fundamental EMF and torque. In this case the 11/12 pitch is a very efficient compromise, it achieves noticeable 5th harmonic reduction at the cost of less than 1\% in fundamental performance.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Q2: Fractional-Slot Winding Design}
The assigned design for this project has the following parameters:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Number of stator slots: $N_s = 15$
    \item Number of rotor poles: $N_m = 14$
    \item Number of phases: $m = 3$
    \item Slots per pole per phase: $N_{spp} \approx 0.357$
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Phase Angles of Induced Voltage per Slot}

The electrical angle of a slot can be found as:
\[\varphi_k = \frac{N_m \cdot 180^\circ}{N_s} \cdot k = \frac{14 \cdot 180^\circ}{15} = 168^\circ\ \cdot k\]
where $k$ is the slot index starting from 1. This means each slot's induced EMF will have a phase angle that is a multiple of $168^\circ$. Since $168^\circ$ is not an integer divisor of $360^\circ$, the slot phasors will be distributed around the circle in a non-repetitive manner, which is characteristic of fractional-slot windings. \subsection{Fundamental Harmonic (\texorpdfstring{$n=1$}{n=1})}

Phasor diagram for the fundamental harmonic ($n=1$) is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:phasor_three_phases}. The slot phasors for each phase are summed vectorially to find the resultant phasor for that phase. The angles of the resultant phasors for the three phases are $0^\circ$ for Phase A, $120^\circ$ for Phase B, and $240^\circ$ for Phase C, confirming a balanced three-phase system.

\begin{figure}[H]

    \label{fig:phasor_three_phases}
\end{figure}

The winding factor for the $n$-th harmonic is computed as the ratio of the vector sum magnitude to the arithmetic sum magnitude of the slot phasors for one phase.
\[k_{w,n} = \frac{\left|\displaystyle\sum_{\text{Phase}} \vec{e}_{k,n}\right|}{N_s / m}\]
where each phasor $\vec{e}_{k,n}$ has unit magnitude. The Phase A phasors and their effective angles are presented in the Table~\ref{tab:phasors_phase_a1} below. Distribution factor for the first fundamental is calculated using the angles of the phasors.

\midrule

The vector sum of these 5 unit phasors is calculated by summing their real and imaginary components:
\[\sum \cos\theta = \cos(0^\circ) + \cos(24^\circ) + \cos(12^\circ) + \cos(348^\circ) + \cos(336^\circ) = 4.783\]
\[\sum \sin\theta = \sin(0^\circ) + \sin(24^\circ) + \sin(12^\circ) + \sin(348^\circ) + \sin(336^\circ) = 0\]
\[k_{w,1} = \frac{|\,\vec{e}_1 + \vec{e}_{14} + \vec{e}_{15} + \vec{e}_2 + \vec{e}_3\,|}{5} = \frac{4.783}{5} = \mathbf{0.957}\]

To verify that this fractional-slot double-layer layout represents a valid and balanced configuration, the net complex fundamental EMF voltage vectors ($\vec{E}_k = \text{Sign} \times e^{j \varphi_k}$) are computed for each phase using the baseline slot angle formula $\varphi_k = (k-1) \times 168^\circ$:

\begin{align*}
    \vec{E}_A &= 2e^{j0^\circ} - 2e^{j168^\circ} - 1e^{j216^\circ} + 2e^{j24^\circ} - 2e^{j192^\circ} = \mathbf{8.662 \angle 9.3^\circ} \\
    \vec{E}_B &= -1e^{j336^\circ} + 2e^{j144^\circ} - 2e^{j312^\circ} + 2e^{j120^\circ} - 2e^{j288^\circ} + 1e^{j96^\circ} = \mathbf{8.662 \angle 129.3^\circ} \\
    \vec{E}_C &= -1e^{j96^\circ} + 2e^{j264^\circ} - 2e^{j72^\circ} + 2e^{j240^\circ} - 2e^{j48^\circ} + 1e^{j216^\circ} = \mathbf{8.662 \angle 249.3^\circ}
\end{align*}

Evaluating the relative displacement between adjacent phases yields:
\begin{align*}
    \Delta\theta_{AB} &= 129.3^\circ - 9.3^\circ = 120.0^\circ \\
    \Delta\theta_{BC} &= 249.3^\circ - 129.3^\circ = 120.0^\circ \\
    \Delta\theta_{CA} &= 369.3^\circ - 249.3^\circ = 120.0^\circ
\end{align*}

The phase resultants form a balanced three-phase set. They possess completely uniform vector magnitudes and maintain an exact angular displacement of $120.0^\circ$ electrical degrees, confirming the electrical balance of the double-layer design.

Stator outer radius is given as $R_{so} = \SI{50}{mm}$, and the rotor-to-stator radius ratio is $0.62$, which allows us to calculate the rotor outer radius:
\[R_m = 0.62 \times R_{so} = 0.62 \times 50 = \mathbf{\SI{31.0}{mm}}\]
The stator inner radius is then:
\[R_{si} = R_m + g = 31.0 + 1.0 = \mathbf{\SI{32.0}{mm}}\]
The air-gap mean radius is the average of the rotor and stator radii:
\[R_{ag} = \frac{R_m + R_{si}}{2} = \frac{31.0 + 32.0}{2} = \mathbf{\SI{31.5}{mm}}\]

The teeth/slot width ratio is selected as $d = 0.62$, which allows to find outer radius of the slot, the height of the slot and the stator back-core thickness:
\[R_{slot,o} = \frac{R_{si}}{d} = \frac{32.0}{0.62} = \mathbf{\SI{42.67}{mm}}\]
\[h_s = R_{slot,o} - R_{si} = 42.67 - 32.0 = \mathbf{\SI{10.67}{mm}}\]
\[h_{core} = R_{so} - R_{slot,o} = 50.0 - 42.67 = \mathbf{\SI{7.33}{mm}}\]

Furthermore, the stator slot pitch is calculated as:
\[\tau_s = \frac{2\pi R_{ag}}{Q} = \frac{2\pi \times 31.5}{15} = \mathbf{\SI{13.40}{mm}}\]
If the teeth/slot width ratio is selected as $d = 0.62$, then the tooth body width and slot width are:
\[w_{th} = d \cdot \tau_s = 0.62 \times 13.40 = \mathbf{\SI{8.31}{mm}}\]
\[w_s = \tau_s - w_{th} = 13.40 - 8.31 = \mathbf{\SI{5.09}{mm}}\]
The slot area is:
\[A_{slot} = w_s \cdot h_s = 5.09 \times 10.67 = \mathbf{\SI{54.33}{mm^2}}\]
The copper area per slot is:
\[A_{cu} = k_{wc} \cdot A_{slot} = 0.60 \times 54.33 = \mathbf{\SI{32.60}{mm^2}}\]
The ampere-turns per slot is:
\[NI_{slot} = J \cdot A_{cu} = 5 \times 32.60 = \mathbf{\SI{163.0}{A}}\]
Finally, the electrical frequency at the rated speed is:
\[f_e = \frac{N_m \cdot n}{120} = \frac{14 \times 1500}{120} = \mathbf{\SI{175}{Hz}}\]

\subsection{Electrical Loading}

The specific electric loading $\bar{A}$ is the total RMS ampere-turns
per unit length of the air-gap circumference:

\begin{equation}
    \bar{A} = \frac{NI_{slot} \cdot Q}{\pi D_i}
            = \frac{163.0 \times 15}{\pi \times 0.063}
            = \mathbf{\SI{12{,}4}{kA/m}}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Carter's Coefficient and Effective Air-Gap}

Carter's coefficient corrects for the increase in effective reluctance
caused by stator slot openings. With selected slot opening $b_0 = \SI{5.0}{mm}$
and $g = \SI{1}{mm}$:

\begin{equation}
    \gamma = \frac{b_0/g}{5 + b_0/g}
           = \frac{5/1}{5 + 5}
           = 0.5
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
    k_c = \frac{\tau_s}{\tau_s - \gamma\, b_0}
        = \frac{13.40}{13.40 - 0.5 \times 5.0}
        = \mathbf{1.23}
\end{equation}

Since the rotor has surface-mount magnets with no slots, the rotor
Carter's coefficient is unity ($k_{c2} = 1$). The effective mechanical
air-gap is then the physical air-gap multiplied by the stator Carter's coefficient:

\begin{equation}
    g' = k_c \cdot g = 1.23 \times 1 = \SI{1.23}{mm}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Effective Axial Length}

Fringing flux at the two axial ends extends the effective active length
by approximately one air-gap length per side:

\begin{equation}
    L_{eff} = L_s + 2g = 100 + 2\times 1 = \mathbf{\SI{102}{mm}}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Air-Gap Peak Flux Density}

Applying Ampere's law around a flux path that traverses the magnet and
the effective air-gap (cylindrical approximation, iron reluctance
neglected):

\begin{equation}
    B_{g,peak}
        = \frac{B_r \cdot l_m/\mu_r}{g' + l_m/\mu_r}
        = \frac{1.3 \times 4/1.05}{1.23 + 4/1.05}
        = \frac{4.952}{5.04}
        = \mathbf{\SI{0.983}{T}}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Magnetic Loading}

The specific magnetic loading is the average air-gap flux density over a
full pole pitch. Because the magnet covers only the fraction $\alpha_p$
of each pole, the remaining arc contributes zero flux:

\begin{equation}
    \bar{B} = \alpha_p \cdot B_{g,peak}
            = 0.889 \times 0.983
            = \mathbf{\SI{0.874}{T}}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Specific Machine Constant and Tangential Shear Stress}

The local tangential stress at the rotor surface is the product of the
local electric and magnetic loading. For unity power factor operation:

\begin{equation}
    \sigma_{tan} = \frac{\bar{A}\cdot\hat{B}_{g}}{\sqrt{2}}
                 = \frac{12{,}4 \times 0.983}{\sqrt{2}}
                 = \mathbf{\SI{8{,}59}{kPa}}
\end{equation}

This value is consistent with the expected range for a small
high-performance PMSM (\SIrange{5}{15}{kPa}). The specific machine
constant, combining winding design, electric loading, and magnetic
loading, is:

\begin{equation}
    C = \frac{\pi^2}{\sqrt{2}}\,k_{w1}\,\bar{A}\,\hat{B}_{g1}
      = \frac{\pi^2}{\sqrt{2}} \times 0.957 \times 12{,}4 \times 1.228
      = \mathbf{\SI{57{,}3}{kW\cdot s/m^3}}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Expected Torque}

The torque is related to the shear stress and the rotor volume:

\begin{equation}
    V_r = \pi R_m^2 L_{eff}
        = \pi \times (0.031)^2 \times 0.102
        = \SI{3.079e-4}{m^3}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
    T = 2\,\sigma_{tan}\,V_r
      = 2 \times 8{,}59 \times 3.079\times10^{-4}
      = \mathbf{\SI{5.29}{Nm}}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Flux Per Pole and Number of Turns}

The pole pitch and the flux per pole are:

\begin{equation}
    \tau_p = \frac{\pi D_i}{N_m}
           = \frac{\pi \times 0.063}{14}
           = \SI{14.1}{mm}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
    \Phi_p = B_{g,peak}\cdot\alpha_p\cdot\tau_p\cdot L_{eff}
           = 0.983 \times 0.889 \times 14.1\times10^{-3} \times 102\times10^{-3}
           = \mathbf{\SI{1.26}{mWb}}
\end{equation}

To select the number of turns, the target phase back-EMF is derived from
the \SI{48}{V} DC bus. Assuming maximum modulation index for the
three-phase inverter:

\begin{equation}
    E_{phase} = \frac{V_{dc}}{\sqrt{3}}
              \approx \SI{27.7}{V_{rms}}
\end{equation}


\begin{equation}
    N_s = \frac{E_{phase}}{\sqrt{2}\,\pi\,f_e\,k_{w1}\,\Phi_p}
        = \frac{27.7}{\sqrt{2}\times\pi\times175\times0.957\times1.260\times10^{-3}}
        \approx \mathbf{30\ \text{turns/phase}}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Tooth Flux Density Verification}

All air-gap flux entering one slot pitch must pass through the
corresponding tooth cross-section. The average flux density in the tooth body is:

\begin{equation}
    B_{tooth} = \frac{\bar{B} \cdot \tau_s}{w_{th}}
              = \frac{0.874 \times 13.40}{8.31}
              = \mathbf{\SI{1.409}{T}} \approx \SI{1.41}{T} \quad \checkmark
\end{equation}
